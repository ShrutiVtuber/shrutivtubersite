## What was actually wrong

**1. The newsletter could be subscribed to and never sent.** The largest find,
and the hardest to see: the subscribe form, double opt-in, one-click
unsubscribe, preference centre and public archive were all built and all
worked. Nothing anywhere wrote a row to the `issue` table. The site was
collecting confirmed addresses against a promise of one letter a month it had
no way to keep.

Worse, `newsletter-issue.html` was the designer's mock-up shipped verbatim —
a September 2026 issue with three fabricated article titles and two paragraphs
of prose she never wrote — and every footer link, including the one-click
unsubscribe, pointed at `{{site_url}}`.

Built: `/admin/newsletter` (write, save, test-send to herself, send), real
per-subscriber unsubscribe and preferences links, batched sending. Three rules
enforced in code: **send once**, **a sent issue's words cannot change**, and
**only confirmed, non-unsubscribed, unpaused addresses** are written to.

**2. She could not see or erase a single subscriber.** She is the controller of
that list. An emailed erasure request had no answer but "wait for a developer".
Erasing deletes the row rather than marking it unsubscribed — those answer two
different requests — and leaves the consent records, which are the evidence.

**3. An unconfigured video provider was improvising a URL.**
`_cloudflare()` fell back to `customer-placeholder.cloudflarestream.com` and
returned `ready: True`. A paid lesson set to Cloudflare would show an empty
player and say nothing, indistinguishable from a video still encoding — while
the module's own docstring already promised the opposite. The admin dropdown
offered Cloudflare too, looking exactly as real as the working option. It now
lists what the server says is configured and marks anything unprotected.

**4. A signed-in reader could not sign out.** `/api/account/signout` existed and
nothing called it; the only sign-out on the site was the admin's. A thirty-day
session on a borrowed laptop with no way to end it.

**5. The question box had no box.** Endpoint, model, honeypot and admin inbox
all built; no way for anyone to ask. Now on `/contact`, deliberately separate
from the contact form — that one gets a private reply, this one may be answered
in public, and the copy says so before you type.

**6. Transits were never built rather than switched off.** The daemon had
computed them all along; the site never passed a nativity. Fixed before the
audit began, at her request.

## Deliberately not built, and why

- **`saved_chart`, `nativity`, `consent_record`, `lesson_progress`,
  `enrolment`** have no admin browsing surface. That is the design: they are
  other people's data and she has no business reading them row by row.
- **Schedule entries, fan works and videos** are not seeded. A placeholder
  stream is a person turning up on a Thursday for nothing; a placeholder fan
  work attributes art to somebody who does not exist. They keep their empty
  states, which is what those states are for.
- **Cloudflare Stream signed playback** is unbuilt on purpose — it needs a
  signing key created through their API, a thing to set up on the day. It now
  refuses instead of improvising.

## Left for her, not code

- **`credit` and `profile_field` are empty on production** (5 and 8 rows
  locally, 0 there). The About page renders its empty state correctly. This is
  content to write, not code to build.
- `/press` says "No collaborations to list yet" — same.
- **`SHRUTI_ADMIN_EMAIL` unset** on production: harmless, the admin is claimed
  in the database instead.
- **`SHRUTI_PASSKEY_ORIGINS` unset**: falls back to `site_url`, which is the
  real origin, so passkeys work.
- **`SHRUTI_SHOP_SHIP_TO` unset**: means worldwide, which is the honest reading
  of "I have not said".

## Second pass, same day — structured data

Found while comparing the site against professional VTuber sites.

**7. The journal was discarding the structured data it exists to produce.**
BeeRanked writes correct schema.org for every page it syncs — `BlogPosting`,
`BreadcrumbList`, `Organization`, with URLs already pointing at
shrutivtuber.com and her real profiles in `sameAs`. The journal route read only
`<main>` and dropped every block. The pages built specifically to be found were
telling search engines the least about themselves. Now passed through verbatim,
parsed first so a malformed block is dropped rather than emitted.

**8. The home page carried no structured data at all** — only `/videos` had
any. Without a `Person` with `sameAs`, nothing outside the site knows that the
name, the Twitch channel and the GitHub account are one person, so the profiles
compete in search results instead of reinforcing each other. Added, home page
only.

Adding it caused a 500 on the front page for one build: the block was written
above the `socials` variable it reads. Caught by re-running the whole-site page
walk, which is the reason that sweep exists.
