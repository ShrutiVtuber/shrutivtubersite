# SEO and marketing audit — 26 August 2026

Audited **as though the holding page were already down**, as asked. Auditing
with `Disallow: /` in force only ever produces one finding — "nothing is
indexed" — and teaches nothing about the site underneath it.

Everything below was measured against the built production image, not against
source. Where I have not verified something, it says so.

---

## 0. The short version

The site is in unusually good technical shape for a personal site: canonical
tags everywhere, a generated sitemap and robots that flip themselves when the
holding page comes down, **zero third-party subresources on every page**, and
time-to-first-byte between 14 and 83 ms locally.

What was wrong was not the plumbing. It was three things:

1. **The share loop could not unfurl.** The feature built to go viral was
   blocked from every platform it was built for. Fixed.
2. **The instruments — the only part of this site a stranger would search for
   without having heard of her — had no hub, no structured data, and thin
   pages.** Partly fixed; the writing is the remaining work.
3. **The admin panel had a Tools screen that edited a table nothing read.**
   Fixed, and it turned out to be the fourth instance of a pattern this project
   keeps producing.

---

## 1. The headline finding: the share loop was blocked

**`/compatible` is the viral feature. Its share links could not show their
cards on X, Discord, Facebook or Slack.**

A comparison is shared as `/chart/compare/s/<token>`, and the card it unfurls
with is `/api/charts/compare/s/<token>/card.png`. Production `robots.txt` said:

```
Disallow: /api/
Disallow: /chart/
```

Those two lines cover both. X, Discord, Facebook and Slack all read robots.txt
before fetching a link to build its preview, so every shared comparison landed
on a timeline as a **bare grey link with no image**.

Nothing about this is visible from the inside. The page returns 200. The card
renders correctly when you open its URL. The `og:` tags are right, and there is
a deliberate comment in the share page explaining that it is *not* `noindex`
because it is the page people post. Every test passed. The only place the
failure appears is on somebody else's timeline, at which point the post is
already spent.

This is the same failure shape as the August audit: **a feature made of working
halves.** Grep finds nothing, because there is nothing marked incomplete.

### The second half of the finding

Blocking `/chart/` was also the *weaker* way to protect the chart pages, which
was the reason it was there — those pages carry somebody's birth data drawn.

A URL disallowed in robots.txt can still be listed in a search result from links
alone. The crawler is forbidden to **fetch** it, so it never reads the `noindex`
that would have kept it out. `noindex` is the mechanism that actually works, and
it only works on a page a crawler is allowed to read. Every private chart page
already sends it.

So the block was costing the share loop everything and buying no privacy.

**Fixed:** `/chart/` is no longer disallowed, `noindex` does the work, and
`Allow: /api/charts/compare/` sits under the broader `Disallow: /api/` so the
card is reachable and the rest of the JSON is not. Guarded by
`backend/tests/test_robots_allows_shares.py`, which resolves the rules the way
RFC 9309 says a crawler does — longest match wins — and asserts the four shared
paths are crawlable while `/admin`, `/account` and `/signin` are not. Reverting
the old rule makes four of its twelve checks fail.

## 2. Cloudflare is rewriting robots.txt, and you should decide whether you want that

Production `robots.txt` is **not what this repo generates.** Cloudflare is
injecting a managed block ahead of it:

```
User-agent: *
Content-Signal: search=yes,ai-train=no,use=reference
Allow: /

User-agent: Amazonbot        Disallow: /
User-agent: Applebot-Extended Disallow: /
User-agent: Bytespider       Disallow: /
User-agent: CCBot            Disallow: /
User-agent: ClaudeBot        Disallow: /
User-agent: Google-Extended   Disallow: /
User-agent: GPTBot           Disallow: /
User-agent: meta-externalagent Disallow: /
```

Two things follow, and neither is an emergency:

- **Search is unaffected.** Googlebot and Bingbot are not blocked, and the
  content signal says `search=yes`. Indexing works normally.
- **AI crawlers are blocked by a default you did not set.** This is Cloudflare's
  managed list, not a choice you made.

**Worth an actual decision, because your situation is unusual.** The instruments
answer exactly the kind of question people now ask an assistant instead of a
search engine — "what is the planetary hour right now", "what does the Attic
calendar say today", "what is the tithi". Being *citable* in an AI answer may
turn out to be a larger channel for this site than blue links, and the pages are
already the honest, working, well-sourced version of those answers.

Against that: `ai-train=no` is a real reservation of rights and blocking training
crawlers is a defensible position that many creators hold.

Note the distinction the managed list already gets right: **GPTBot** (training)
is blocked, but **OAI-SearchBot** and **ChatGPT-User** are not — so ChatGPT can
still fetch and cite your pages. The same is not true of ClaudeBot.

I have not changed this. It is a values decision, not a bug.

### On finding 2, because it is a pattern and not an accident

The `tool` table has existed since the site was designed. It is in the admin's
`EDITABLE` registry. It has a field spec. It had six rows in it. **No page has
ever read a single one.**

Meanwhile the name, subtitle, glyph, one-line description and the whole "how it
is reckoned" passage for each instrument lived as literals inside the `.astro`
files, and the landing page kept a *third* copy with its own shorter blurbs.

So the copy most likely to need changing was in the one place she could not
change, and the screen that looked like it changed it did nothing at all.

I checked the other ten editable tables the same way. **`Tool` was the only
orphan** — `Section`, `Project`, `SocialLink`, `ProfileField`, `Credit`,
`ScheduleEntry`, `FanArt`, `Sponsor`, `Outfit` and `CardDesign` are all read by
a public route and consumed by a page.

The rows are now the source, seeded by **generating the migration from the
pages** rather than retyping them — retyping four hundred words of prose nine
times is how a migration quietly rewrites somebody's writing.

Two fields, not one, because they do different jobs:

- **`summary`** — the line under the heading, the card on `/tools`, and the
  meta/og description. One row, three places.
- **`landing_blurb`** — the shorter, more inviting line on the front page,
  written for somebody who has not decided to care yet. Falls back to `summary`.

Plus `glyph`, `native`, `category` and `reckoned`, all editable.

One literal remains and it is deliberate: the **"Day at a glance"** card on the
landing page points at `/today`, which is a page rather than an instrument.
Giving it a row would put a card on `/tools` that leaves `/tools`. Say the word
and I will make it editable another way.

## 5. What is genuinely strong, and worth not breaking

Measured, not asserted:

- **Zero third-party subresources on every page tested** — `/`, `/tools`, a
  tool page, `/compatible`, `/today`, `/community`, `/shop`, `/about`. No
  hosted fonts, no embeds, no pixels, no CDN.
- **TTFB 14–83 ms** locally; HTML 18–51 kB.
- **Canonicals on every page**, from configuration rather than from
  `Astro.url`, which reports `localhost` behind Caddy.
- **`Person` + `WebSite` with `sameAs`** on the home page — the single
  highest-value tag a creator site carries, because it is what ties the name,
  the Twitch channel and the GitHub account together instead of letting them
  compete.
- **RSS on the journal**, discoverable from `<head>` on every page.
- **robots.txt and the sitemap flip themselves** when the holding page comes
  down. Nothing to remember on cutover.

The zero-third-party property is worth defending explicitly, because almost
every SEO and marketing recommendation you will read assumes you will trade it.

## 6. What not to do

- **No third-party analytics, ever.** You already have first-party cookieless
  counting that answers the two questions you actually asked — which tools get
  used, and how people move through the site. GA4 would answer the same
  questions worse and cost the property above.
- **No tracking pixels**, including the Meta and TikTok pixels every "grow your
  audience" guide recommends.
- **No AI-written filler on the instrument pages.** The one asset those pages
  have against astro-seek is that a person who understands the reckoning wrote
  them. Diluting that to hit a word count trades the only advantage for the one
  thing everybody else already has.
- **No fake ratings.** `Product`, `Course` and `WebApplication` all accept
  `aggregateRating`, and inventing one is the single thing in this audit that
  gets a site penalised rather than merely ignored. None of what I added claims
  a rating.
- **No engagement dark patterns** — no fake scarcity on the shop, no streak
  guilt in the community features.

## 8. The competitive picture

A research pass measured the incumbents. **Read the caveats first**, because
they change how much weight each number carries.

**Not verified:** SERP *ordering* (the competitor set is right; the ranking is
from a search tool, not from Google). **No keyword volumes** — Ahrefs and
Semrush are paywalled and Google Trends returned HTTP 429. **No Core Web
Vitals** — the PageSpeed API was over quota on every attempt. Traffic figures
are Similarweb estimates and are unreliable below ~50K/month. Domain ages are
authoritative (RDAP). Page weights and ad counts were measured live.

Anyone quoting you a "domain authority" number is guessing — it is a
proprietary third-party score, not something Google publishes.

### The single best piece of news, and it is from the primary source

Google's **Quality Rater Guidelines** (11 September 2025 edition) mention
astrology exactly once, in a carve-out:

> "pages should not be considered to contain Harmfully Misleading Information
> if they exclusively contain: ● **Non-YMYL** content created with a clear
> entertainment purpose… Examples include many types of fiction, satire or
> parody, **astrology**, folklore, myths, and urban legends."

Astrology is **explicitly Non-YMYL and explicitly carved out** of Harmfully
Misleading Information. There is no Highest-E-E-A-T bar to clear, and the site
cannot be penalised for astrology being unfalsifiable.

**Two limits, and they are real.** The carve-out requires no hard claims of
factual accuracy, and requires the content not be harmful. An electional page
advising on **surgery, medication or investments** walks straight out of the
carve-out and into YMYL. Worth knowing before the horoscopes get specific.

This also happens to vindicate the disclaimers already on the site.

| **Isopsephy** | Weak. A free-host `.epizy.com` page and a **plain-HTTP** page sit in the top ten. |
| **Sigil generator** | Weak. Leader at ~12K/month and *declining*; slots wasted on Pinterest and itch.io sub-pages. |
| **Attic calendar** | **Vacant** — and worth close to zero traffic. Win it for credibility, not visitors. |

**Soft, 6–12 months:** planetary hours, isopsephy, Attic calendar, sigil
generator, Western-framed lunar mansions.
**Medium, 12–18 months:** synastry, transits, English-language pañcāṅga aimed
at the US and the diaspora.
**Do not chase:** natal chart head terms, Hindi/Tamil panchang, solar terms.

**One caution I am passing on unchanged:** "solar stations" was never verified
as a search phrase and may have no demand at all. Do not build a content
strategy on that page's title until it is checked.

### The incumbents are beatable, and specifically so

Measured live, today:

- **alabe.com ranks #1 for "free natal chart"** on Bootstrap 3.3.2 (January
  2015), with a 153 KB logo, 58-second visits, and a viewport tag reading
  `initial scale=1` — an invalid value, silently discarded. The most vulnerable
  page in the study is holding the most valuable position.
- **drikpanchang.com** ships 2,419 KB over 97 requests, and has a **broken
  default location** — it renders Brussels or Berlin for a US visitor, while
  **23% of its traffic is American.**
- **cafeastrology.com**'s tool page is 237 KB, of which **161 KB (68%) is the
  AdSense script.** Still growing, though — do not mistake ugly for dying.
- **astro.com** puts a **registration wall before any chart.**
- **astrolibrary.org is the model, not the target**: 201 KB, **zero ads**,
  185K/month, entirely on long-tail terms like "rising sign calculator" and
  "midheaven calculator". That is the shape this site should grow into.

### New sites do win this niche, in months

`augurine.com` was registered **six months ago** and is already on two
planetary-hours SERPs. `panchangbodh.com` is **13.5 months old** and does
~300K/month at 79% organic.

**Cold water, twice.** panchangbodh won **in Hindi** — that door is not open to
an English site. And `planetaryhours.org` ranks **position 1** for "planetary
hours today" while sitting at a global rank of 9.8 million with 28-second
visits. **Ranking is not traffic.** If anyone ever shows you a rank-tracker
screenshot as proof of success, this is the counter-example.

### AI Overviews, and what it means for how the writing is structured

Zero-click is 68% of US searches (Jan–Apr 2026), and Ahrefs measured **−34.5%
CTR at position 1 when an AI Overview is present**. Critically, **99.2% of
AI-Overview-triggering keywords are informational.**

**No study isolates calculator queries, so this next part is inference and I am
marking it as such.** An AI Overview cannot compute *your* chart, or the
planetary hour in *your* city, right now. Every growing tool site in the study
grows on organic search — astrolibrary 73%, sigilengine 76%, panchangbodh 79%.

If that inference is right, it has one concrete consequence:

> **The tool must BE the page.** Do not split the explanation into separate
> blog articles. An explainer article is squarely in the −34.5% zone; a working
> instrument with its explanation attached is not.

Which is an argument for exactly what §4 recommends — deepen the instrument
pages themselves — and against the more obvious "start a blog about planetary
hours" plan.

### The Gen-AI channel, with the number attached

Similarweb now reports **"Gen AI" as a discrete traffic channel**, and it is
the **third-largest** for several sites in this exact niche right now.

This is the cost side of §2's decision, and the precision matters:

- **Google-Extended costs nothing in Search.** Google states verbatim that it
  "does not impact a site's inclusion in Google Search nor is it used as a
  ranking signal in Google Search." Blocking it is free.
- **GPTBot and ClaudeBot carry the real price** — they are the ones that would
  otherwise feed the channel above.
- The rest (Bytespider, CCBot, Amazonbot, Applebot-Extended, meta-externalagent)
  are approximately free to block.

Your current stance — `search=yes, ai-train=no, use=reference` — is coherent and
defensible. I am not recommending you abandon it. I am recommending you hold it
**as an informed choice with the number attached**, rather than as a Cloudflare
default you never chose.

### The moat, and it is specifically hers

Of the new entrants winning this niche — augurine, planetaryhours.org,
asknovaastrology, panchangbodh — **not one has an author, an About page, or a
credential between them.**

A named practitioner who shows the working is the thing programmatic AI content
cannot fake. And the Quality Rater Guidelines confirm astrology is not
credential-gated, so the usual E-E-A-T ceiling does not apply here.

That moat is available to this site and to almost nobody else in the space. It
is also, not coincidentally, what the `reckoned` passages already do.

1. **Deepen `/tools/planetary-hours` first.** Soft SERP, real demand, and the

2. **Then isopsephy and the sigil generator.** Both have genuinely weak top

5. **Do not write separate explainer articles.** Put the explanation on the
   instrument.
6. **Then** synastry, transits, and English-language pañcāṅga.

## 10. One more robots.txt oddity, verified

Production `robots.txt` currently contains **two `User-agent: *` groups**:
Cloudflare's injected `Allow: /`, then ours with `Disallow: /` for the holding
page. Merged as RFC 9309 says a crawler merges them, `Allow` and `Disallow` at
`/` are the same length — and a tie goes to **Allow**.

So the holding page's `Disallow: /` is **not actually blocking anything.** The
only reason the unfinished site is not in the index is the `<meta name="robots"
content="noindex">` on the holding page, which I verified is present.

Which is the same lesson as §1 arriving from the other direction: **`noindex` is
the mechanism that works, and robots.txt is not.** No action needed — the
correct thing is already what is protecting the site — but it is worth knowing
that the robots line was never doing the job it appeared to be doing.
