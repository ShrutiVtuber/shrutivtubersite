Nothing was ever retrograde

A chart correct in every particular except one, and a zero that looked exactly like an answer.

By Shruti · 25 August 2026
Ephemeris Bugs

A chart was coming out right in every particular except one. Every planet was direct — all of them, at every date I tried, in every house system. A birth chart with no retrogrades is not wrong the way a crash is wrong. It is wrong the way a stopped clock is wrong: it reads plausibly, twice a day.

The shape of the bug

Swiss Ephemeris fills the speed slot only when it is asked to. Ask for a position without FLG_SPEED and you get a position, correct to the arcsecond, along with a zero where the daily motion should be.

xx, ret = swe.calc_ut(jd, swe.MOON, swe.FLG_SWIEPH)
speed    = xx[3]        # 0.0 — never computed
retrograde = speed < 0.0

Zero is not less than zero. So nothing was ever retrograde — not because the arithmetic was wrong, but because the input was a plausible-looking zero rather than a missing value.

A number that was never computed should not be able to reach a comparison. If it can, the comparison is not the bug.

Why it survived

Nothing threw. No test failed, because the tests asserted positions and the positions were right. The Moon's daily motion read 0.00°/day, which looks like a formatting problem rather than an absence, and I read it as one for two days.

What the flags actually return

Flag combinationLongitudeDaily motionRelative cost
FLG_SWIEPHexact0.00°/day1.0×
FLG_SWIEPH | FLG_SPEEDexactcomputed1.4×
FLG_MOSEPH | FLG_SPEED±1″computed0.6×

The test that would have caught it

One assertion over a date I already knew by heart: Mercury stations retrograde on 9 August 2026, and any build that says otherwise is not asking for speed. It is now the first test in the suite, and the only one whose failure I have already seen.

The fix, and the rule

- xx, ret = swe.calc_ut(jd, body, FLG_SWIEPH)
+ xx, ret = swe.calc_ut(jd, body, FLG_SWIEPH | FLG_SPEED)
+ if not flags & FLG_SPEED:
+     raise Unreckonable("speed was not requested")

The one-line change is the flag. The change that matters is the second one: the ephemeris layer now refuses to hand back a field it never computed. Unreckonable is the same exception the calendars raise at the pole on a day the sun does not rise — the instruments would rather say οὐ λογίζεται, it is not reckoned, than return a number nobody derived.

A note on daily motion

The Moon's motion runs from roughly 11°/day to 15°/day and never rests. Sanskrit astronomy names the retrograde condition वक्री (vakrī, crooked) — a description of apparent motion, not a verdict on it. Zero was never a plausible value for either tradition to report.

What changed in the instrument

The chart page now prints ℞ beside a retrograde body, states the flag it ran under in its provenance card, and shows an em dash with a footnote where the speed field is absent. A chart that cannot know is more useful than a chart that guesses.

The month that would not open

Two reckonings, two first days, and an instrument that had to learn to say both.

By Shruti · 25 August 2026
Calendars

The conjunction fell at 21:07 on a Sunday. The crescent that should have opened Μεταγειτνιών was, that evening, four degrees above a horizon the sun had only just left — technically present, practically invisible, and by the rule the corpus actually uses, not yet a month.

Two rules, one sky

Conjunction reckoning starts the month at a computed instant. Observed-crescent reckoning starts it at the first evening the new moon can be seen from a named place. They agree most months and part company two or three times a year, and when they part company they do not disagree slightly: they disagree by a whole day, and every festival in the month moves with them.

The Athenians did not know the instant of conjunction. They knew whether someone had seen it.

What the page does now

It shows both, marks which one it is counting by, and refuses to pick. Where the two rules give different dates the header carries them side by side rather than resolving to the one I happen to prefer.

The stream where it broke

I found this live, on a Thursday, with about forty people watching me insist the calendar was wrong for eleven minutes before reading my own rule text.

Casting a chart

updated 2026-08-25 · by Shruti edit this page →

The ephemeris is a small HTTP service. Every instrument on this site is a page over it, and it answers the same questions for anyone who asks.

What you need

A moment and a place. The moment is a date and a time; the place is a latitude and a longitude. Nothing else is required, and nothing else is stored.

GET /chart?date=1990-05-04&time=14:20&lat=37.9838&lon=23.7275
     &zodiac=both&ayanamsa=lahiri&houses=whole-sign

Omit time and the service does not guess noon. It answers with the day's positions, marks the houses null, and says why — an unknown birth time is a real condition, not a validation error.

What comes back

Both traditions, side by side and unranked. The tropical zodiac and the sidereal one are not two attempts at the same answer; they are two different questions, and the response gives you both rather than choosing.

FieldTropicalSidereal · LahiriNote
sun14°02′ ♉︎20°11′ ♈︎Ayanāṁśa 23°51′
moon02°47′ ♑︎08°56′ ♐︎उत्तराषाढा · Uttarāṣāḍhā
moon.speed13.42°/day13.42°/dayPresent only with the speed flag
asc28°19′ ♌︎04°28′ ♌︎Whole-sign houses follow the sign
atticἑβδόμη ΜουνιχιῶνοςObserved crescent, Athens

Why nothing is marked correct

Ranking them would be a claim about which sky is real. The instrument's job is to reckon under a stated rule and name the rule; the practitioner's job is to know which rule they are working in.

When it refuses

A refusal is a first-class response with its own shape. It carries the condition, the rule that produced it, and the alternatives that would answer — never a fabricated number.

{
  "error": "unreckonable",
  "condition": "no_sunrise",
  "rule": "sunrise = upper limb clears the horizon",
  "detail": "The sun does not rise at 78.22°N on 2026-12-21.",
  "alternatives": ["civil_twilight", "solar_midnight", "previous_sunrise"]
}

The instruments render this state rather than an empty table; on a tool page it is the cannot-compute panel, and here it is documented as a normal outcome. A day with no sunrise is not a bug in the calendar. It is a fact about latitude.

The Attic festival corpus

revised 2026-08-24 · 14 sources · signed Soror Eu. A.

The corpus is the table the Attic calendar reads from: which festivals fall on which day of which month, and how firmly each of those claims can be made. It is scholarship with a schema, and the schema has to carry doubt without hiding it.

How days are graded

Three grades and one refusal. A grade describes the strength of the evidence for the date, not the importance of the festival — the Mysteries are the best-attested rite in the corpus and their opening day is still argued about.
Legend ●attested — dated by a primary source ◐reconstructed — inferred from a cycle ○disputed — sources conflict
Grade Festival Month Day Source
●attested Noumenia ἑκάστου μηνός 01 Plut. Mor. 828a
● Deipnon of Hekate ἑκάστου μηνός 30 · ἕνη καὶ νέα Ar. Plut. 594
◐ Metageitnia Μεταγειτνιών c. 07–12 inferred · cycle
○ Greater Mysteries, opening Βοηδρομιών 14 or 15 two authorities
— Skira Σκιροφοριών not dated by us see note 3

Five of the fifty-one entries carry no day. Where the corpus will not date something the instrument shows the month and stops; it does not offer a likeliest guess, and the calendar page marks the day as unassigned rather than empty.

What the instrument does with a grade

The festival column on the calendar page shows the same three glyphs, in the same order of certainty, with the same legend. A reader who learns them here can read them there — and a disputed date renders as both candidates, not as the one I find more convincing.
