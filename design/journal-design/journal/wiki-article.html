The Attic festival corpus

revised 2026-08-24·14 sources·signed Soror Eu. A.

The corpus is the table the Attic calendar reads from: which festivals fall on which day of which month, and how firmly each of those claims can be made. It is scholarship with a schema, and the schema has to carry doubt without hiding it.

How days are graded

Three grades and one refusal. A grade describes the strength of the evidence for the date, not the importance of the festival — the Mysteries are the best-attested rite in the corpus and their opening day is still argued about.

Five of the fifty-one entries carry no day. Where the corpus will not date something the instrument shows the month and stops; it does not offer a likeliest guess, and the calendar page marks the day as unassigned rather than empty.

What the instrument does with a grade

The festival column on the calendar page shows the same three glyphs, in the same order of certainty, with the same legend. A reader who learns them here can read them there — and a disputed date renders as both candidates, not as the one I find more convincing.
