Casting a chart

updated 2026-08-25·by Shruti edit this page →

The ephemeris is a small HTTP service. Every instrument on this site is a page over it, and it answers the same questions for anyone who asks.

What you need

A moment and a place. The moment is a date and a time; the place is a latitude and a longitude. Nothing else is required, and nothing else is stored.

GET /chart?date=1990-05-04&time=14:20&lat=37.9838&lon=23.7275
     &zodiac=both&ayanamsa=lahiri&houses=whole-sign

Omit time and the service does not guess noon. It answers with the day's positions, marks the houses null, and says why — an unknown birth time is a real condition, not a validation error.

What comes back

Both traditions, side by side and unranked. The tropical zodiac and the sidereal one are not two attempts at the same answer; they are two different questions, and the response gives you both rather than choosing.

Why nothing is marked correct

Ranking them would be a claim about which sky is real. The instrument's job is to reckon under a stated rule and name the rule; the practitioner's job is to know which rule they are working in.

When it refuses

A refusal is a first-class response with its own shape. It carries the condition, the rule that produced it, and the alternatives that would answer — never a fabricated number.

{
  "error": "unreckonable",
  "condition": "no_sunrise",
  "rule": "sunrise = upper limb clears the horizon",
  "detail": "The sun does not rise at 78.22°N on 2026-12-21.",
  "alternatives": ["civil_twilight", "solar_midnight", "previous_sunrise"]
}

The instruments render this state rather than an empty table; on a tool page it is the cannot-compute panel, and here it is documented as a normal outcome. A day with no sunrise is not a bug in the calendar. It is a fact about latitude.
