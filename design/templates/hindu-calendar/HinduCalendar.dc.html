This year carries an adhika māsa — a thirteenth month inserted when a lunar month contains no solar transition, keeping the moons tied to the seasons. It takes no festivals of its own: they wait for the nija month that follows. The rarer opposite, a kṣaya māsa, drops a month entirely and happens roughly twice a century.

◐ Two answers, both defensible

The authorities disagree on this date

The tithi ends close to sunset, and the rule for which day owns a festival is not the same everywhere. Two published panchangs put Dīpāvalī on two different evenings. The instrument shows both rather than choosing one and pretending the question is settled.

How it is reckoned

A lunar month is one full cycle of the Moon against the Sun; a solar month is the Sun crossing one sidereal sign. Twelve lunar months fall about eleven days short of twelve solar ones, so a month that contains no solar transition is doubled and the year runs to thirteen. The arithmetic is old, exact, and regional — which is why two calendars printed the same year can disagree without either being wrong.
