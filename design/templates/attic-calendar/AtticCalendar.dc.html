Attic calendar

Ἀττικὸν ἡμερολόγιον

The lunisolar month as Athens kept it — twelve moons, an intercalary thirteenth when the year drifts, and days counted toward the moon rather than away from it.

When the month begins

The Athenians began the month at the first visible crescent; the arithmetic that survives them begins it at the conjunction. The two can differ by two days, which is enough to move a festival.

Outside the attested range

Before 432 BCE the Metonic cycle was not yet in use, and the intercalations Athens actually made are not recoverable from the evidence. The moon can be computed for any date; the calendar cannot. The instrument will not fabricate an archon year.

How it is reckoned

Each month opens at the noumenia and runs full or hollow — thirty days or twenty-nine — as the moon requires. Twelve of them fall short of the solar year by eleven days, so a thirteenth is inserted seven times in nineteen years. Athens ran a second calendar alongside it for the council, out of step on purpose, which is why the city could hold two dates at once without embarrassment.
