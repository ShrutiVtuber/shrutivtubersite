In six scripts, a letter is also a number — so a word is also a figure. The traditions do not agree on the values, and one of them does not produce a sum at all. All six are here, unmerged.

Which reckoning

Six systems, six letter tables, and no conversion between them. Pick the one the source uses rather than the one that gives the answer you were hoping for.

Devanagari is the odd one. Kaṭapayādi assigns a digit to each consonant and reads the string as a place-value number, so it has no total to compare — a word encodes a figure rather than summing to one. The instrument shows it as what it is instead of forcing it into the Greek shape.

Equal sums are a coincidence until an argument is made for them. The instrument finds them; it does not claim they mean anything. Matches are only ever within one system — a Greek sum and a Hebrew sum of the same value are not a correspondence.
